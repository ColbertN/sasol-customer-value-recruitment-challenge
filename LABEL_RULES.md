# Label rules

All settings below are implemented in label_rules.py and label_config.json.

1. At any cutoff, eligible customers have at least three distinct TransactionId values strictly before it. Counts include all transaction types/categories. Eligibility never depends on future purchases.
2. Use the next three calendar months as outcomes and the preceding three calendar months as the growth comparison. Adoption checks ALL observed history before the cutoff, not only the previous quarter. A qualifying purchase has positive Quantity and TotalExclAmount; count distinct baskets, not lines.
3. Named adjustment items TOTAL/ROUNDING DISCO, PENSIONER DISCOUNT and WOW_PROMO do not contribute to targets or purchase activity. All rows remain in the historical release.
4. Fuel regression: sum signed Quantity where Level2=Fuel and LineItemType=Sale_Fuel. This is litres. Three nonstandard source Fuel/Sale rows are not included in this regression. Non-fuel regression: sum signed TotalExclAmount outside Fuel, Value added services, Print media and Carrier Bags. Use source pre-tax store amounts; do not subtract TotalModifierAmount again or sum repeated basket totals. Genuine product returns reduce net totals. The literal category value "NA" (distinct from a blank/missing cell — see `DATA_DICTIONARY.md`) and "Unknown" count as real purchase activity: they contribute to CLV_nonfuel, count toward basket eligibility, and keep a customer active rather than Inactivity — a customer whose only outcome-window purchase was in NA or Unknown is not Inactivity. This is different from the three excluded categories above (Value added services, Print media, Carrier Bags), which count as no activity at all. NA/Unknown purchases can still win an opportunity, but — like Krispy Kreme or Car Wash in rule 7 — they always map to "Other." No customer target is clipped: negative totals fail the generator pending an explicit policy; none occur in the tested calibration/final snapshots.
5. Opportunity candidates exclude the same three non-fuel categories and adjustment items. For each original Level2 category, growth requires an observed historical purchase, at least one future purchase, net spend growth >=R20, AND future spend at least 25% higher than the previous-quarter comparison spend (both conditions must hold). Adoption requires no prior qualifying purchase, at least one future purchase and positive future net category spend.
6. Compare qualifying candidates in source monetary amounts: growth increment = future minus previous-quarter net category spend; adoption increment = future net spend. Fuel opportunity increments use source TotalExclAmount, NOT litres; the source fuel amount includes tax while store amounts are pre-tax. Highest monetary increment wins; equal increments prefer adoption, then alphabetical original category. A previously observed category with no recent-quarter purchases may qualify as growth on return.
7. Categories compete individually for the winning increment *before* any Other-mapping is applied — never after. For example, Krispy Kreme and Car Wash both map to "Other" for adoption, but they compete separately: if a customer's biggest new-category spend was R80 on Krispy Kreme and R30 on Car Wash, the winner is Krispy Kreme (R80), then relabeled to "New category adoption: Other." They are never pooled together first (which would let R80 + R30 = R110 beat a real R100 category it shouldn't have beaten). A prior purchase in one Other-mapped category does not block adoption of a different original category. Exact mapping is in `label_config.json`.
8. Inactivity means no qualifying in-scope purchases in the outcome. Stable means there was activity but no qualifying growth/adoption candidate; declining customers can be Stable. Return-only activity is not a positive purchase.
9. Regression normalization: s is the population standard deviation (ddof=0) of ln(1+y) over the fixed five historical quarterly snapshots listed in the config. Transform z=ln(1+y)/s, without centering. Fixed public constants apply to all submissions; participants do not recalculate them from their own windows. Customers may appear in several calibration snapshots. Constants use no final-quarter outcomes.

One combined Opportunity label is submitted per customer; no separate opportunity/category columns. Class names and spelling are authoritative in the config.
