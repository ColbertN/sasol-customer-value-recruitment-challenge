I was confused by this at first too. I don't think it's a bug. The two RMSE numbers aren't going into the formula raw. Each one gets turned into a "higher is better" score first, otherwise a worse RMSE would push your total up.

From my own submissions it looks like they do:

- fuel: 1 − RMSE / 0.74

- non-fuel: 1 − RMSE / 0.816

then weight those 0.3 / 0.3 / 0.4 with the F1.

Plugging in your numbers:

- fuel: 1 − 0.5931/0.740 ≈ 0.1985

- non-fuel: 1 − 0.7237/0.816 ≈ 0.1131

- total: 0.3 × 0.1985 + 0.3 × 0.1131 + 0.4 × 0.5122 ≈ 0.2984

which matches your 0.2983. The 0.740 and 0.816 are just what I backed out from my own scores, so I'm not sure where they come from (maybe some baseline RMSE). It would be nice if Zindi could confirm and add it to the evaluation page.

Thanks for the check - this isn't a bug, and your math is actually right for the formula you assumed. The discrepancy comes from a step upstream of that formula: each metric is normalised before being combined, not combined directly in its raw form.

This challenge uses Zindi's multi-metric evaluation - the general approach is explained there. In short: because RMSE has no fixed upper bound (a value of 0 is a perfect score, but it can grow arbitrarily large) while Weighted F1 is naturally bounded between 0 and 1, the platform rescales the regression metrics onto a comparable footing before applying the 0.3 / 0.3 / 0.4 weighting. That's why the raw CLV Fuel, CLV Non Fuel, and Weighted F1 Opportunity numbers you see aren't what actually gets weighted-averaged - the formula in the description refers to the normalised versions of those metrics, not the raw displayed values.
